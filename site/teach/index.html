For instructors

A ready session for a finance or investments class. Students need a browser and nothing else.

What students learn

  • Sharpe ratio: return earned per unit of risk, above the cash rate.
  • Diversification: why blending two imperfectly correlated funds can lower risk.
  • Money-weighted vs time-weighted return: how the timing of deposits changes the answer, and when each is the right measure.

A 45-minute session

  1. 0 to 5 min: frame. Ask: if two portfolios both returned 8%, are they equal?
  2. 5 to 15 min: sample portfolio. Open the sample portfolio (an invented student). Compare it with the benchmark and read the headline result together.
  3. 15 to 30 min: the Sharpe lesson. Students step through How Sharpe works, using their own mix or the sample.
  4. 30 to 40 min: change the mix. In pairs, change the weights, predict the effect on risk and return, then check.
  5. 40 to 45 min: debrief. What did a high return hide? Why is a short history a weak guide?

What students need

A web browser. No install, no account and no account data: they can use the sample portfolio or type in a mix of funds. Everything is worked out in the browser and nothing is uploaded.

Caveats to say out loud

Monthly data, before taxes and costs, US funds only. The tool is information for learning, not investment advice.
