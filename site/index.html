Use your own prices

Load a CSV of month-end (or daily) adjusted prices, so dividends are counted. Either one column per fund (date,VTI,BND) or three columns (date,symbol,price). The file is read in your browser and never uploaded. Blank cells are fine: a fund that started later just shortens the shared period. A single-fund download from a price site (Open, High, Low, Adj Close) is not this layout.

How to read this

What the numbers mean
Worth at the end
What the money would be worth on the last date, before taxes and fees, if every deposit followed your weights.
Return per year
How fast the investments grew each year, as if $1 had stayed in the whole time. Deposits do not flatter it. Shown from 12 months of data.
Total return
The same measure over the whole period, not per year.
Ups and downs (volatility)
How much monthly returns swing, as a yearly figure. Higher means a bumpier ride.
Worst fall
The biggest drop from a previous high point.
Sharpe ratio
Return above a cash rate for each unit of ups and downs. Higher is better. Here the cash rate is a flat assumption (3.5% a year with the demo funds, and the same with your own file).
Assumptions and limits
  • Prices are month-end and adjusted for dividends. Fund fees are already inside fund prices; trading costs and taxes are not modeled.
  • Deposits happen at month-end, after that month's return. Resetting to your weights happens at month-end with no cost or tax.
  • The benchmark is one fund with the same starting amount, deposits and way of managing.
  • Past results do not predict future ones. A few years is a short sample, and one good or bad year can dominate it.
